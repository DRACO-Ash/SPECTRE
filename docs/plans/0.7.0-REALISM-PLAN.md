# 0.7.0: realistic intercepts, planned in plain language

## What is wrong today

The Intercept Engine answers the question you type in, not the question you
want answered. You give it one coast time, one time of flight (TOF), one
revolution count, one hop count and a 500 m/s budget, and every method is
solved once with exactly those values. Four things follow, each measured on
the GEO test pair (FACT):

● **The answer depends on a guess.** Lambert costs 59 m/s with a 6 hour TOF
  and 9 m/s with 18 hours. The panel shows whichever TOF you typed, and
  nothing tells you a cheaper one exists.
● **Rows that never reach the target are ranked as threats.** Bi-elliptic
  "costs" 587 m/s and ends 80,000 km from the target; it is listed HIGH.
  The threat sweep has refused such rows since 0.6.7; the engine never did.
● **Min Time always spends the whole budget.** That is its definition: the
  fastest arrival for a given ΔV. At 500 m/s it spends 500, at 50 it spends
  50. Its ΔV measures the budget, not the threat.
● **500 m/s is not a realistic budget.** See the evidence below.

You also cannot read off the burn point, the coast, the TOF, the
revolutions or the closest approach for each row. The engine has them; the
table does not show them.

Checking every method on the GEO test pair while planning found two more
(FACT):

● **Four miss distances are the request, not the result.** R-bar hop, CW
  drift, COLA and Formation report the standoff they were asked for (5, 10,
  1 and 5 km) with the target 599 km away. CW drift spends 1 m/s, which
  moves the red satellite about 22 km in 6 hours. Lambert and Min Time
  report the miss they aimed for too. 0.6.7 fixed this for phasing, the hops
  and the plane change only.
● **Three methods are not red approaches at all.** Formation and Evasion are
  blue's defensive burns (their own documentation says blue evades red), and
  COLA is a collision-avoidance burn that moves away. All three are ranked
  today as if they were red threats.

## What the NOTSOs say

Source: the JCO TACREP_NOTSO export you supplied, 3,124 messages from 28
April 2022 to 27 March 2026. It is a superset of the 500 already shipped in
`spectre/data/notso_cache.json` (every one of those 500 is in it).

How the figures were made: every stated ΔV ("Delta V: 3.7 m/s", "a delta-v
of 9.45 m/s") was read from single-object messages, and the same burn
repeated in later follow-ups was counted once. Each burn was then sorted:

● **Launch phase**: a climb from transfer orbit to GEO. The text around it
  says GTO, circularise, inject, insert, perigee raise or apogee burn.
  These are 20 to 1,000 m/s. They are not operations against a satellite.
● **Uncertain**: JCO itself says the figure is unreliable ("high
  uncertainty", "sparse observations"). Left out.
● **On station**: everything else. 959 burns by 129 satellites.

| On-station measure (FACT, from the export) | Value |
|---|---|
| A single burn: median | 1.4 m/s |
| A single burn: 95 in 100 are at or below | 12 m/s |
| A single burn: largest seen | 76 m/s |
| Busiest 14 days per satellite: median satellite | 8.8 m/s |
| Busiest 14 days: 9 in 10 satellites stay at or below | 36.8 m/s |
| Busiest 14 days: largest seen, any satellite | 171.8 m/s (Cosmos 2589) |
| Next largest | 152.4 (Object C, 64627), 124.3 (TJS-13), 121.4 m/s (Tianlian 1-03) |

Cosmos 2589's figure was checked by hand: 14 on-station burns between 24
November and 5 December 2025, in a 24-hour orbit (period 1,436.7 minutes),
with no launch-phase wording near any of them. It is a real operation.

Two objects you will recognise (FACT):

● **TJS-19 (63924)**, the red satellite in your screenshot: 999.6, 461 and
  176 m/s while climbing to GEO in May 2025; 21 m/s in its busiest 14 days
  since.
● **SJ-23 (55131)**: 305 to 558 m/s per burn reaching GEO in January 2023;
  21 m/s in its busiest 14 days since.

The earlier research put the largest documented on-station operation, SJ-21
towing Beidou-2 G2, at about 120 m/s round trip. The NOTSO figure of 171.8 m/s
is consistent with it and slightly above.

**Limits (INFERENCE):** the burn date is taken as the day JCO first reported
it, usually within a day of the burn. Two genuinely different burns of
exactly the same size on the same object would be counted once. A burn that
NOTSOs never reported is not counted, so these are floors on what objects
have done, not ceilings on what they could do.

## The rule the engine will apply

Every solved row gets one of three realism bands, from its ΔV:

| Band | Meaning | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Seen before** | This satellite has spent this much on station before | ΔV at or below its own busiest 14 days |
| **Possible** | No sign this satellite has done it, but some satellite has | ΔV at or below 171.8 m/s, the largest 14-day on-station total in the record |
| **Unrealistic** | Nothing in the record has spent this on station | Above 171.8 m/s |

A satellite with no on-station NOTSO history has no "Seen before" band; its
rows are Possible or Unrealistic.

Separately, a row that ends more than 50 km from the target is **not an
approach**, the same rule the sweep uses.

A red approach whose flown closest approach is beyond that distance is not
an approach. The distance is 50 km unless the panel's "Approach within"
field sets another.

Unrealistic rows and non-approaches are **discounted**: they stay visible,
dimmed, below the red approaches that count, with the reason in the Realism
column and the expanded row, and they never set the worst-case headline or
the most likely course of action.

The 171.8 m/s figure is computed from the evidence file, not typed into the
code, so a newer NOTSO export moves it.

## The phases

### Phase 1: turn the NOTSOs into evidence

● A new module reads NOTSO records and produces, per satellite: number of
  on-station burns, largest single burn, busiest 14-day total, date of the
  last burn, and largest launch-phase burn. Plus the fleet figures above.
● A script turns your export into a small evidence file shipped with the
  app (`spectre/data/manoeuvre_evidence.json`). It holds catalogue numbers,
  satellite names and figures only: no message text, no analyst names, no
  email addresses.
● At run time the same module also reads the app's NOTSO cache, which UDL
  sync keeps adding to, so a manoeuvre reported after this export still
  counts.
● The raw zip stays out of the App Store package: the packager already
  deletes every `.zip` it stages.

### Phase 2: realism bands

● A new module assigns each row its band and states the reason in a
  sentence, for example "Possible: 165.0 m/s is more than TJS-19 has spent
  on station in any 14 days (21.0 m/s), but within the most any satellite
  in the record spent on station in 14 days (171.8 m/s, by COSMOS 2589)".
● Min Time is run at a realistic budget instead of the panel's maximum:
  36.8 m/s, what 9 in 10 satellites stay within over 14 days, or the
  satellite's own busiest 14 days if that is more. A satellite's own record
  is a floor (SJ-21 has two NOTSO burns, 1 m/s, yet towed a satellite), so
  it can raise the budget but never lower it. Its row then answers "how fast could it
  get there spending what it realistically spends", and says so.

### Phase 2b: measure every miss by flying the burns

● Every red approach row has its burns flown: the red satellite is
  propagated to its first burn, each burn is applied in turn, and the
  closest approach to the target is measured from the first burn to
  arrival (over one red orbit for a single burn with no arrival time, such
  as a plane change). That measured figure replaces whatever the solver
  reported, so no row can claim a miss it does not achieve.
● All 23 solvers build their burns through the same function in the same
  inertial frame (checked line by line), so one checker serves them all.
● The table splits into three groups:
  ■ **Red approaches**: ranked, banded, and the only rows that can set
    the worst case.
  ■ **Avoidance and defence**: COLA, Evasion and Formation, shown as blue's
    options and never ranked as red threats.
  ■ **Assessments**: the scored methods (manoeuvre detect, intent,
    stability, fingerprint, terrain, detectability, intercept envelope, and
    J2 drift, which reports the RAAN gap natural precession closes, in
    degrees), unchanged.

### Phase 3: let the engine search instead of guessing

For every method that has a free choice, the engine tries the options and
keeps the cheapest one whose flown closest approach is within the approach
distance (50 km unless set):

| What is searched | Options tried |
|---|---|
| Burn point | Now, and the red satellite's next apogee, perigee, ascending node and descending node within one orbit |
| Time of flight | 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2 and 3 red orbits |
| Revolutions (phasing) | 1 to 6 |
| Hops (V-bar, H-bar) | 1 to 5 |

Which method searches what:

| Method | Burn point | TOF | Revolutions | Hops |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lambert, R-bar hop, CW drift | ● | ● | | |
| Phasing | ● | | ● | |
| V-bar hop, H-bar hop | ● | | | ● |
| Hohmann, bi-elliptic, plane change, NMC, GEO drift, Min Time | ● | | | |
| Avoidance and defence, and the assessments | unchanged: not red approaches | | | |

● If no option reaches the target, the row shows the closest one and is
  marked not an approach.
● Your typed values stay available: set Targeting to "Use the values typed
  below" and the engine runs exactly what you typed, as today.
● V-bar and H-bar hops are searched but not flown: their solvers list only
  the first and last burn of a multi-hop plan (the deferred 0.6.7 item), so
  their closest approach is the solver's own estimate, and the row says so.
● Time cost, measured: one Lambert solve takes 22 ms, so 6 burn points by
  7 TOFs is under a second, and the other methods take under 3 ms each.
  Measured on the GEO test pair, the whole searched batch takes 0.35 s
  against 0.03 s typed. It runs off the web server's main thread, as the
  batch does today.

### Phase 4: show it

A headline names the **most likely course of action**: the cheapest red
approach that is Seen before or Possible, with its burn point, ΔV, TOF and
closest approach. That answers "where would it burn" in one line.

Each row gains, in the table or its expanded detail:

● **Burn point**: its name and time, for example "Apogee, 31 Oct 07:15 UTC".
● **Coast**: wait from the manoeuvre start to the burn.
● **TOF**: burn to arrival.
● **Revolutions** or **hops**, where the method has them.
● **Closest approach** to the target, in km.
● **Realism**: the band and its sentence.
● **Options tried**: how many combinations the search compared.

Form changes:

● **Targeting**: "Search for the cheapest approach" (the default) or "Use
  the values typed below".
● **Max ΔV** defaults to 171.8 m/s, the record's largest, instead of 500. You
  can still type any figure; realism bands are judged against the evidence
  whatever you type.
● **Target Dist** is relabelled "Approach within (m, 0 = 50 km)": how close
  a red approach must come to count. The proximity methods still aim for it
  as their standoff, as before.

### Phase 5: prove it and package it

Tests (all on fixed epochs, per CLAUDE.md, so none depends on the hour):

● Evidence: launch-phase, uncertain and on-station burns sorted correctly
  from records written in each of the export's phrasings; a burn repeated
  in a follow-up counted once; a multi-object message not attributed; the
  14-day window; the shipped file is consistent and holds figures and
  names only.
● Realism: each band at and either side of its boundary; no history means
  no "Seen before"; the sentence names the figures.
● Flown miss: a Lambert transfer flies to within a few km of its target;
  CW drift's flown miss is the real distance, not the 10 km it was asked
  for; COLA, Evasion and Formation never appear among red approaches.
● Search: Lambert finds a cheaper TOF than the default 6 hours on the test
  pair; the chosen burn point, coast and TOF are reported; a method that
  never arrives is marked not an approach; manual mode runs exactly the
  typed values; Min Time no longer spends 500.
● Panel: unrealistic and non-approach rows never set the worst case; every
  new field has a label; the form posts the new switch.

Code Quality gate: `sonar_rules.py`, `check-quality.sh` and the
cognitive-complexity check against 0.6.11 (the last release the gate
scored), changed-line coverage at or above 80%, and the review agent.
The route already has 13 parameters, the gate's limit, so the new switch
goes into a dependency rather than a fourteenth parameter.

CVE: no new dependency and no Dockerfile change. The Grype step in
`verify-container.sh` runs on the built image before packaging.

## What this release does not do

● The threat sweep and asset screening keep their 500 m/s default for
  now. Bringing them onto the same evidence and bands is the natural
  0.7.1, once you have seen the engine's behaviour.
● Realism is fleet-wide, not per orbit regime. The top 14-day totals come
  from HEO and GEO objects alike, and the record is too thin per regime to
  split it honestly.
● The evidence does not model propellant remaining. A satellite near end
  of life is screened out by 0.6.8's working-life rule, not by this.
